01

The film in ninety seconds

Moira is eighteen. She arranges her own kidnapping on the Baja coast to pull a few million out of the father who sold her as a child — and hires her best friend's cousins, four brothers who drove four days to get there, to play the men who took her.

It works. Her father pays inside a day, without hesitating, because his insurer covers the loss before it clears. Then he makes a second call, to the thirty-year kidnap-and-ransom man he retained to bring her home, and asks him to make certain that nothing — nobody — comes back across the border. There is a trust in her name, and it is ironclad on every point but one.

It assumes she outlives him.

By sundown the house on the hill is a room with a gun in every hand, and the only thing that walks out of it intact is the money.

The audience is braced for the kidnappers to turn. They do. Nobody is braced for the father.

The Precedent
Case Study

OBSESSION (2026)

Curry Barker was twenty-six, made his name on YouTube, had no industry relationships, and shot his first feature in twenty days for $750,000. Focus Features bought it out of Toronto for $14 million.

It opened to $17.2M on 2,615 screens — and then went up, taking $23.9M the following weekend. A 39.4% second-weekend increase: the largest jump in modern times for a wide release outside the Christmas corridor.

A− CinemaScore. 94% Rotten Tomatoes. Seventh highest-grossing release of the year.

Negative cost$0.75M
Worldwide gross$425M+

The sliver is the budget. That is not a chart trick — it is the actual ratio.

What it means for us

$750,000 is now empirically survivable at wide release

The relevant number in that case study is not $425 million. It is $750,000 — the exact bottom of our range, and a figure the market has just proven can reach a global audience when the specificity is real and the audience is young.

That is a materially different risk picture than it was eighteen months ago. The ceiling is no longer theoretical, and the floor no longer requires a star to clear.

ROSARITO is built for that audience by construction, not by retrofit:

— Every decision that matters is made by someone under thirty. The only two adults in the film are a father who sells his daughter and a man paid to clean up after him.
— The premise survives a fifteen-second vertical cut, which is where this audience is found.
— It is a hard-R crime picture with sex, violence and a bleak ending: the register Gen Z keeps showing up for and the studios have largely stopped supplying.

Three budgets. One of them is half price.

Three budgets have been built line-by-line against this draft, not scaled from a template. Each lands on its number. The only variable between them is talent — and the schedule that talent requires.

Tier One

$750,000

Full discovery cast. SAG Ultra Low Budget. 20–22 days. Two primary addresses, crew engaged locally through a Baja service producer.

The Obsession posture. Downside bounded at the price of a commercial. Highest multiple on the table and the best story attached to it.

Going from $1.5M to $3M costs $1.5M gross — but only $930,708 net of the credit. Roughly 62 cents on the dollar. The raise is not "we need double." It's "the second half is half price."
The incentive

EFICA — 30%, transferable

Mexico replaced EFICINE this year. The new credit (decree 16 Feb 2026, guidelines 30 Mar 2026) is expressly available to a US producer with no Mexican entity, producing through a local service company.

It carries a minimum qualifying feature spend of MXN 40,000,000 — $2,305,476. Neither lower tier can reach it. The $3M clears by $94,524, under 4%. That surplus is the single most important number in the financing.

And the border zone

8% IVA, not 16%

Playas de Rosarito is named by name in Artículo Primero of the northern border decree (DOF, 31 Dec 2018, extended through 31 Dec 2026). Local vendor invoicing runs at 8%.

Two catches the summaries miss: the credit belongs to the vendor, so the question is whether a given rental house is registered and invoicing at 8% — ask for a sample CFDI. And imports are excluded at the full rate, which is one more reason to rent in Baja rather than truck a package down.

The one real risk

Eleven dawns in Room 307

One recurring set. First light through a window over a fog-shrouded Pacific. Roughly twenty usable minutes a morning, eleven separate 4:30am call times, for fragments that may cut to fifteen seconds — and no way to buy back a morning the fog doesn't arrive.

It is the highest-variance line in the schedule at every tier, and it is a textbook in-camera VFX application: a single window looking at a single view. Built against an LED volume, eleven weather-dependent dawns collapse into controllable stage hours with consistent light across the spine of the film.

Budgeted, not discovered

Lines a generic budget would miss

— SEDENA weapons permitting. 27 firearm references; Mexico controls firearms federally and prop weapons cannot cross the border.
— Squib and stunt package for a crossfire that kills five people in one room.
— Dawn marine unit with port captain clearance for the boat sequence.
— Four picture cars against sixteen interior-car scenes.
— A scorpion and rattlesnake wrangler — the first and last images require them.
— Bilingual overhead throughout: script supervision, ADs and safety in two languages.
— An intimacy coordinator, budgeted from prep rather than added under pressure.

06

Why it is not just another crime picture

The script has an actual visual language rather than a mood board — three systems that run clean through it and pay off without anybody saying so out loud.

Symbol One

The statue has a front and a back, and the film knows which is which

A giant Christ stands on the hill between the resort and the hacienda, facing the ocean. Where a character stands relative to His body is where they stand morally — and the script never once states it.

Alex is always in front, looking up: “the giant statue Jesus appears to be looking down on him, judging him for their actions.” Moira is always behind — her hand on the glass “in line with the back of the giant statue,” then “staring at the back of the Jesus statue in front of her.” Quinn drives to the killing and the statue “slides past on his right, its back to him,” then “behind him now, getting smaller in the mirror.” He drives out from under the gaze in order to say yes.

And Memo — eighteen, gentle, shot through the leg — is the only one who speaks to Him: “Jesus please save me. I promise I will devote my life to you if I survive this.” He is the only survivor. He walks off that hill with the entire fortune in his fist and a smile spreading across his face. The prayer is answered in full, and the answer is obscene.

Symbol Two

A scorpion in a rattlesnake's mouth

The film opens on a bottle of tequila with a coiled rattlesnake inside and a tiny yellow scorpion in its mouth — and Moira names it before Alex can finish the sentence. She knows the story before it happens. She has already identified herself.

When the abduction goes off, Alex drops the bottle. It shatters, and in the spilled liquor “the tiny scorpion from the bottle has freed itself from the rattlesnake's mouth” and crawls away in the chaos. The final image of the film, after the car goes over the hill, is a tiny yellow scorpion crawling onto an empty road and crossing it.

The image refuses to resolve, which is why it works. Read one way she is prey, and she only got out because the glass broke. Read the other way, the scorpion is what killed the snake — and it has been sitting inside the corpse of the thing that tried to eat it, preserved in alcohol, waiting. Manolo supplies the moral out loud: we take what we want, it's in our nature.

Symbol Three

The rhyme nobody in the film ever notices

The gift the silver-haired man carries into an eleven-year-old's bedroom is wrapped in “pretty pink paper, a bow tied around it.” Her father says: “This is a friend of mine. He came a long way to meet you. Be a good girl,” and closes the door.

Sixty pages later, four grown men sit around a coffee table talking about what they'll buy. Osie wants a Corvette. Chago says, “maybe a pink Corvette.” They laugh. Manolo — nine children by five mothers — softens and admits “I think I'd look good in that pink Corvette.” When the number doubles, Chago's first words to him are: “looks like you might be able to buy yourself that pink Corvette after all.”

He never buys it. He is dead within twenty pages, killed by the girl whose price would have paid for it. Pink is the colour of what you are given when you are the thing being sold — and the colour of what you dream of buying with the proceeds. The gift and the car are the same object at opposite ends of one transaction. No character connects them. The audience does.

This is a genre picture that behaves like a tragedy, and it costs the same as a commercial.

Roles that start careers

Every part under thirty is a discovery role. The two adult parts are twelve-day jobs for actors whose faces sell a poster. That is the entire casting strategy, and it is why the budget scales the way it does.

MOIRA
18 · Female · Lead

The architect. Rich, watched, and disposable to the only person supposed to want her. A girl performing helplessness for money who discovers she is better at violence than at love. The one thing she cannot fake, on camera, is fear.

ALEX
24 · Male · Lead

The believer. A freelance computer kid from LA who thinks logistics are the same thing as love. Every promise he makes at dawn is broken by the film — and he means all of them when he says them.

BENNY
22 · Female

Moira's friend, first lover, procurer and employee — asked to be all four at once. The most alive presence in the first hour. “You pushed me out and turned me into the help.”

MANOLO
29 · Male

Eldest cousin. Nine children by five mothers, a revolver tattooed on his forearm, and a philosophy he dies proving. The most castable male role in an international market.

QUINN
60s · Male · Name role

Thirty years of kidnap-and-ransom, a flask in his coffee, and the only character who can name what the film is about. Introduced losing a fish. Twelve days on the schedule, top of the one-sheet.

RICHARD
50s · Male · Name role

Never raises his voice, never crosses the border, never says the sentence out loud. Not a monster of appetite — a monster of accounting.

MEMO
18 · Male

The youngest cousin and Moira's structural twin. Prays out loud in a firefight. The only one who walks off the hill — bleeding, smiling, holding everything.

What we are not hiding

EFICA is a recoupment event, not committed financing — the credit is expected roughly eighteen months after wrap, and the programme has no completed track record. The $3M tier clears its threshold by under 4%, and there is no partial award. The comparables above are survivorship: films selected because they broke out.
